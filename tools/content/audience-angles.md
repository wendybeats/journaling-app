# Audience-test video angles — who bites?

*2026-08-26 · Segmentation test: one story-shape per suspected audience,
problem-first, the app named only in the final beat. Format held
constant (founder-to-camera or text-over-b-roll, mellow delivery,
intensity in the claim) so AUDIENCE is the only variable. Tag each post
in the Live log with its persona code — Friday's Eden breakdown then
segments results by code.*

Reading the test: **saves + comments per reach**, follower composition
shifts, and DM content. A segment that "bites" = its two angles both
overperform. One angle per 2-3 days; never two different personas the
same day (muddies attribution).

## RET — The Returner (in therapy, fills journals, never rereads)

**RET-1 · "The $200 silence"** — hook principle: named cost.
You pay real money for a therapy hour, and spend the first fifteen
minutes of it trying to remember your own week. The week HAPPENED — it
just didn't leave a trace. Escalate: you performed "fine" so well all
week that even you believed it. Turn: what changed wasn't writing more,
it was having the week HANDED BACK. Final beat: "My journal reads me
back now — one line a day. It's called Endpaper."

**RET-2 · "Venting isn't processing"** — hook principle: kill a
sacred cow. Everyone says "journal about it." So you do. And it helps
for exactly one evening, because writing something down and UNDERSTANDING
it are different acts. A notebook is write-only memory. Escalate: ten
notebooks in a drawer, zero pages ever reread. Turn: the value was never
the writing — it's the reading back nobody does. Final beat: app.

## FDR — The Overthinking Founder

**FDR-1 · "The 3am board meeting"** — hook principle: name the secret
ritual. Nobody at your company knows the real decision meeting happens
at 3am, in your head, with one attendee, every night — and the same
agenda item never resolves. Escalate: the loop follows you into the
shower, the drive, the middle of other people's sentences. Turn: a loop
dies when it's pinned to a page it can't rewrite. Final beat: "I pin
mine in Endpaper. Sealed at midnight. The loop stays on yesterday's
page."

**FDR-2 · "Where do you put the thoughts that aren't safe anywhere?"**
— hook principle: the paranoid question. Your notes app syncs to a
server. Your docs have an AI reading them for "suggestions." Your
real thinking — the doubts, the numbers, the people problems — deserves
a room with no one in it. Escalate: so it stays in your head, which is
the worst room of all. Final beat: no AI, no account, on-device — that's
the whole architecture.

## AES — The Quiet-Luxury Aesthete

**AES-1 · "The unconsidered corner"** — hook principle: taste
contradiction. Your morning is edited: the ceramic cup, the pour-over,
the linen, the light. Then you open your inner life in a notes app that
looks like a spreadsheet. The most intimate thing you do is the least
considered object you own. Turn: it should feel like the shelf, not the
office. Final beat: the app, shown not described — ink on bone, one
quiet page. (This is the pool/hotel/first-class b-roll lane.)

**AES-2 · "The most expensive thing you own"** — hook principle:
reframe luxury. It's not the watch or the trip — it's attention, the
one thing everyone photographing the pool doesn't have. Ten quiet
minutes that are actually yours is the scarcest good on earth. Turn:
guard it like something expensive, because it is. Final beat: "Ten
minutes, one page, sealed. Endpaper." (First-class window at night,
pool before anyone's up.)

## NCW — The New-Chapter Writer

**NCW-1 · "The middle has no witnesses"** — hook principle: the unnamed
truth. When the big thing happens — the baby, the ending, day one of
recovery — everyone asks how you are. Month four, nobody asks. The
middle of becoming someone else is completely unwitnessed. Turn: so
witness it yourself. One honest line a day is enough. Final beat: the
journal that keeps the record and hands it back. (Dawn-kitchen visuals,
NOT the luxury lane — per the workshop stress-test.)

**NCW-2 · "You won't remember this year the way you think"** — hook
principle: memory is unreliable. Hard seasons blur — the mind sands
them down to survive them. In two years you'll tell one flat sentence
about the year that rebuilt you. Escalate: the details that made it real
are evaporating weekly. Turn: the record has to be kept while it's
happening, and kept somewhere it can't be edited later. Final beat:
sealed pages — "who I was becoming, in ink."
