# Endpaper — Product Rundown for Marketing

**Date:** July 30, 2026 · For an experienced marketer brainstorming UGC
and distribution. Everything here is true and shippable-as-claimed; the
"can't claim" list at the bottom matters as much as the pitch.

---

## One paragraph

Endpaper is a paid iPhone journal that does almost nothing on purpose.
You open it, today's page is waiting with the cursor ready, you write,
you close it. What you write can never be edited or deleted — like ink.
Each day you write, a dot fills in; a year becomes a constellation you
can hold. Once a week (and month, and year) it can read your own words
back to you — the topics you kept returning to, quoted verbatim. No
account, no server, no analytics, no AI chatbot. $39.99/year after a
7-day free trial. "The journal that's just a journal."

## The three claims that carry everything

1. **Permanence.** No edit, no delete, anywhere in the product. This is
   the emotional hook — writing that *counts*. Every competitor lets you
   curate; Endpaper is the only one that behaves like paper.
2. **Privacy by architecture, not policy.** There is no server. Writing
   lives on-device and in the user's own iCloud. The maker *cannot* read
   your journal — it's not a promise, it's a topology. App Store privacy
   label: "Data Not Collected."
3. **Reflection without AI.** Weekly/monthly/yearly reviews are
   deterministic — your own recurring words, counted and quoted back.
   Never advice, never analysis, never a chatbot. In 2026 this is
   counter-positioning gold: *the journal that doesn't want to talk to
   you about your feelings.*

## Product moments that are inherently content

- **The dot matrix.** The year calendar — 365 dots, filled days in ink —
  is the screenshot. It's a streak visual with no gamification: no
  numbers, no fire emoji, just accumulation. Progress-porn that reads as
  restraint.
- **The reorganizing dots.** Tapping a month makes every dot in the year
  physically fly and reassemble into that month; tapping a day scatters
  all but one. Screen-records beautifully; nothing else in the category
  moves like this.
- **The year review ("Wrapped for your interior life").** Days written,
  words written, the word that kept surfacing, your first line of the
  year quoted back. Spotify-Wrapped-shaped and deeply personal.
- **The permanence flinch.** The moment a tester realizes they can't
  delete what they wrote is a *felt event* — testers mention it
  unprompted. "I typed something honest and then realized it was
  permanent" is a UGC script that writes itself.
- **The look.** Bone paper, ink text, three typefaces, zero icons, zero
  color. Screenshots are identifiable at feed-scroll speed.

## Audience (hypotheses, in order)

1. **Lapsed journalers** — people with four abandoned notebooks and two
   abandoned apps; the pitch is "the app got out of the way."
2. **Digital minimalists / quiet-tech** — the anti-app app; adjacent to
   dumbphone / analog-revival content, which is thriving UGC territory.
3. **Morning-pages / stoic-practice crowd** — journaling as discipline;
   permanence and the daily dot map directly onto practice culture.
4. **Privacy-conscious** — smaller but loud; "no server" is a spec they
   verify and evangelize.

## Competitive frame

Day One ($49.99, the feature-rich incumbent), Journey ($49.99), Stoic
($39.99, prompts+AI), Reflectly ($59.99, AI), Apple Journal (free, the
floor). Endpaper's position: **under the incumbent, priced beside the
practice apps, and the only one selling *less*.** The category sells
features; Endpaper sells a rule. Bear ($29.99) is the register-sibling
in notes: quiet, indie, design-first.

## Commercial facts

- iOS 17+, iPhone-first. $39.99/year, annual only, 7-day free trial,
  hard paywall (nothing works without starting the trial — deliberate).
- "About the price of one good paper notebook" is the sanctioned price
  anchor.
- Solo maker: Wendell Barton. First-person voice is real and available —
  "I built this because…" carries indie-maker distribution (build in
  public, maker communities, product forums).
- Site: endpaper.space (privacy/terms live there). Beta via TestFlight
  now; public launch after beta hardening.

## Voice — how it must sound

Quiet, direct, first person where it's the maker speaking. Complete
sentences. No exclamation points, no feature-matrix language, no
"unlock/supercharge/journey." The product never congratulates and never
nudges; marketing shouldn't either. Think: the copy register of a good
hardcover's dust jacket. Existing samples: the site hero, the App Store
description draft (docs/endpaper-store-listing.md), the TestFlight
welcome note (docs/endpaper-beta-kit.md).

## UGC seed angles (raw material, not a plan)

- "I wrote one honest sentence a day for 30 days in an app that won't
  let me delete anything" — the permanence challenge, creator-native.
- "This app has no AI and that's the point" — counter-programming
  against AI-journal content; duet/stitch material.
- The dot-matrix month-end screenshot as a recurring format ("October,
  honest version") — low-effort, high-identity, repeatable.
- Year-review reveal videos in January (the product's seasonal moment;
  Wrapped-adjacent timing).
- Analog-people conversion arcs: fountain-pen/notebook creators trying
  "the only app that behaves like paper."
- Maker-story content: "I couldn't read your journal if I wanted to —
  here's the architecture" (privacy-tech explainer crossover).

## Constraints the marketer must know

- **No analytics in the app — ever.** It's the product's spine and the
  privacy label. Attribution is App Store Connect referral data, promo
  codes, and site-side link tracking only. No SDK will be added for a
  campaign.
- **No share-image export exists yet.** The dots and year review are
  screenshot-shareable but there's no designed share card. If UGC is the
  strategy, a share-card feature (calendar/wrapped → designed image) is
  the single highest-leverage product ask — raise it and it gets built.
- **Can't claim:** encryption beyond Apple's standard iCloud protection
  ("end-to-end" only if the user has Advanced Data Protection on — don't
  claim it); "AI-powered" anything (there is none, by design); Android
  or web (none planned); editing/undo (the absence is the product).
- **Trademark:** "Endpaper" is a common bookbinding noun; the App Store
  listing name is "Endpaper — Journal" (bare name was taken). Formal
  trademark screen is still pending — flag before any big paid push.
- Beta testers get a 1-year promo code at public launch (grandfathering
  is already decided) — usable as an early-advocate mechanic.
